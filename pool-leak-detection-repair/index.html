Signs

Signs your pool has a leak

Losing more than a quarter-inch of water per day

Normal evaporation typically accounts for a small amount of daily water loss; losing noticeably more, especially with the pump off, points to an actual leak.

Wet spots in the yard near pool equipment

Consistently damp ground near the pump, filter, or plumbing lines running to the pool often means an underground pipe is leaking.

Air bubbles in the return lines

Air bubbling into the pool from a return jet, rather than just at startup, can indicate a suction-side leak pulling air into the system.

Cracks or discoloration on the pool shell

Visible cracks, especially ones that seem to grow, or staining that doesn't correspond to a chemical issue, can point to a structural leak in the shell itself.

Local factors

Pool leak detection in a coastal-adjacent market

West Torrance and South Torrance's single-story homes on standard suburban lots include a real number of backyard pools, most installed alongside the original home or added during a later remodel. Pool plumbing in this market faces the same underlying pressures as household plumbing: naturally hard Los Angeles County water accelerates scale buildup in pool equipment and fittings, and marine-layer humidity speeds up corrosion on above-ground metal components near the coast.

Because a pool's plumbing runs underground between the shell and the equipment pad, a leak there behaves very differently from a leak in a home's water lines, and needs equipment built specifically for pool leak tracing rather than standard household leak detection tools.

How we diagnose it

How we find a pool leak

Bucket test

A simple bucket test compares water loss in the pool against a sealed control bucket, confirming whether loss is beyond normal evaporation before further testing.

Equipment pad inspection

The pump, filter, and associated plumbing at the equipment pad are checked first, since a surprising number of apparent pool leaks turn out to be a fitting or seal failure above ground rather than a buried line.

Pressure testing the plumbing lines

Each plumbing line running to and from the pool is pressure tested individually to isolate which line, if any, is leaking.

Electronic and dye testing

Electronic leak detection and dye testing help pinpoint leaks at fittings, lights, or the shell itself that pressure testing alone can't fully localize.

Confirm before repair

We confirm the leak's exact location and likely cause before recommending a specific repair approach.

Repair options

What happens once the leak is found

A plumbing-line leak is typically repaired by accessing and fixing the specific section of pipe, sometimes requiring a small excavation near the equipment pad or pool shell. A fitting or light-niche leak is often a more contained repair. A structural shell leak may require a specialized pool repair approach beyond plumbing alone, and we'll be direct about when that's the case rather than attempting a repair outside our scope.

Once repaired, we retest to confirm the leak is fully resolved before considering the job complete, rather than assuming a single fix has solved it.

FAQ

Questions about pool leak detection in Torrance

How much water loss is normal for a pool?

Roughly a quarter-inch per day from evaporation is typical in this climate; consistently losing more than that, especially with the pump off, suggests an actual leak.

Can you tell if the leak is in the plumbing or the pool shell itself?

Yes, pressure testing the individual plumbing lines helps isolate whether the leak is in the pipes or points toward the shell or a fitting instead.

Do you repair the pool shell itself, or just the plumbing?

We handle plumbing-line and fitting leaks directly. For a structural shell leak, we'll identify it clearly and can point you toward the right type of specialist if it's outside plumbing scope.

Could the leak just be at the pump or filter, not underground at all?

It's worth checking first. A seal or fitting failure at the equipment pad is a common, much simpler cause of water loss than a buried line leak.
