Redondo Beach housing

From pier-adjacent beach cottages to mid-century inland homes

Redondo Beach's housing splits fairly clearly by distance from the water. Near the pier and King Harbor, many homes date to the 1920s through 1940s, small beach cottages that have often been remodeled but frequently still carry original or early-replacement plumbing runs. Further inland, in North Redondo and toward the Riviera, the housing shifts to mid-century single-story construction more similar in age to Torrance's own tract homes, just built with a shorter walk to the water.

That gradient matters for plumbing, because a beachfront cottage's exposed fittings take a harder hit from salt air and marine humidity than an inland Redondo Beach home does, even within the same city.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Redondo Beach

Redondo Beach's beachfront cottages and older supply lines mean a leak or burst pipe can start fast. Here is what to do the moment you notice one, before help arrives.

Shut off the main valve

Stop the water at the source first, if you can safely reach the main shutoff valve.

Kill power to wet areas

If water has reached an outlet or panel, cut power to that area rather than working around it.

Clear the immediate area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the water while help is on the way.

Call our 24/7 line

A live dispatcher answers any hour and can walk you through the next step while a technician heads your way.

Local FAQ

Questions from Redondo Beach residents

Do beachfront homes really need different plumbing service than inland homes?

The underlying plumbing skills are the same, but a home within a few blocks of King Harbor is worth checking more often for corrosion on exposed fittings than a home further inland.

Is Redondo Beach's water the same hardness as Torrance's?

Yes, both cities draw on the same regional water supply, so the same hard-water scale issues that affect Torrance water heaters and fixtures apply in Redondo Beach as well.

How is Redondo Beach's water utility different from Torrance's?

Redondo Beach is served by California Water Service's Hermosa-Redondo district, which has operated in the area since 1927, rather than Torrance Municipal Water, though both draw on similar regional groundwater and imported supplies.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Redondo Beach?

Yes, we cover both Redondo Beach ZIP codes, 90277 and 90278, from the pier and King Harbor area through North Redondo and the Riviera.

Is emergency service available in Redondo Beach?

Yes, our 24/7 emergency line covers Redondo Beach the same as the rest of our service area, including nights, weekends, and holidays for burst pipes and active leaks.
