Signs

Signs your sump pump needs attention

Pump runs constantly

A pump that never seems to shut off may have a stuck float switch or may be undersized for the water it's handling.

Pump doesn't turn on during rain

If water is visibly rising in the sump pit but the pump isn't activating, the switch or motor has likely failed.

Unusual noises

Grinding, rattling, or humming without the pump actually moving water usually points to a failing motor or debris caught in the impeller.

Visible rust or age

Most sump pumps have a working life of 7 to 10 years. A visibly aged or rusted unit is a good candidate for proactive replacement before it fails.

Local factors

Why sump pump reliability matters in this part of Torrance

Torrance's storm drain system carries most street runoff directly to the ocean, and the city maintains 17 stormwater retention basins to manage heavy flows, but low-lying areas, including the vicinity of the Madrona Marsh nature preserve, still carry real flood risk during major storms. Torrance has also seen documented street flooding on low-lying roads, including near the 110 Freeway, during especially heavy winter rain.

For a property with a basement, crawlspace, or below-grade area, a working sump pump is the difference between a heavy storm being a non-event and it becoming a real water-damage claim, which is why we treat sump pump failures as a genuine emergency, not a routine repair.

In the central part of Torrance, the low ground around Madrona Marsh at Madrona Avenue and Sepulveda Boulevard is where drainage questions are most natural, since the preserve exists because water has always collected there. Properties near that part of the city are the ones we check first for sump discharge paths ahead of storm season.

How we diagnose it

How we diagnose a sump pump problem

Float switch test

We test whether the float switch is triggering the pump correctly at the right water level.

Motor and impeller check

The motor and impeller are inspected for wear, debris, or electrical failure.

Discharge line inspection

A clogged or frozen discharge line can make a working pump look like it's failed. We check the full path water takes out of the pit.

Backup power discussion

For properties in flood-risk areas, we'll discuss whether a battery backup system makes sense given how often power fails during the storms that also trigger sump pump use.

Not sure what's actually wrong

"My basement flooded" or "the pump stopped, I need it fixed quickly"

Sump pump calls are almost always urgent by nature: water is already accumulating, or a storm is coming and a homeowner just discovered the pump isn't working. We treat these calls as the emergencies they are rather than scheduling them like a routine repair.

Water is already coming into my basement. What should I do first?

If it's safe to do so, move valuables away from the water and call us immediately. Do not stand in standing water near electrical equipment.

My pump stopped working and more rain is coming. Can you come out today?

Yes, sump pump failure ahead of forecast rain is treated as an emergency call, and we prioritize it accordingly.

Getting ready for the season

Before the next winter storm arrives

A sump pump is easy to forget about between storms, which is exactly when problems go unnoticed. Testing the pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and confirming it activates and drains properly takes a few minutes and catches most float-switch failures before they matter.

Keeping the discharge line clear of debris outside the home is just as important as the pump itself. A clogged or frozen discharge line can make a perfectly functional pump look like it has failed. We check both the pump and its full discharge path on every service call, not just the unit sitting in the pit.

Pump failure

The pump stopped and water is rising

A sump pump that does not run during heavy rain is an urgent problem. Check that the pump is plugged in and the float moves freely. If water is still rising, call our emergency line and we will plan the fastest response.

Sewage ejector pumps are a different animal. They lift waste from a below-grade bathroom or laundry, and a failed one can back up into the house. If you are not sure which pump you have, tell the dispatcher what is installed.

FAQ

Questions about sump pump service in Torrance

Do you install battery backup sump pumps?

Yes, and for flood-risk properties we generally recommend one, since storms that trigger sump pump use also frequently cause power outages.

Is sump pump repair available at night or during a storm?

Yes, our emergency line covers sump pump failures 24 hours a day, including during active storms.

How long do sump pumps typically last?

Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years. A unit approaching that age is worth having inspected proactively, especially ahead of winter storm season.

Should I get my sump pump checked before winter storms arrive?

Yes. A proactive check before the wet season catches a weak float switch or aging motor while it's a scheduled repair, not a middle-of-the-storm emergency.

Do you replace sump pumps on an emergency basis?

Call the emergency line and describe what is happening. The dispatcher will explain what can be done and how soon.

Can you replace a sewage ejector pump?

Yes. Ejector pumps lift wastewater from low bathrooms or laundry areas, and replacement is quoted after the technician sees the setup.

Water is seeping through my basement wall after a storm. Is that a sump pump issue?

Not always. Seepage through walls is often a foundation or drainage issue rather than a plumbing one. We can tell you if a sump system would help.
