Signs

Signs hydro jetting is the right call

Snaking hasn't solved it

If a line clogs again within weeks of being snaked, the pipe likely has a coating of grease, scale, or root fragments that a snake pushes through but doesn't remove.

Camera shows root intrusion

Visible roots inside a sewer line, confirmed by camera inspection, generally need jetting to clear rather than a mechanical auger alone.

Slow drainage across multiple fixtures

A main line coated in buildup along its length, rather than blocked at one point, often shows up as gradually slowing drainage everywhere.

Commercial kitchen grease buildup

Restaurant and commercial kitchen lines accumulate grease coating that mechanical snaking doesn't fully address, even with a working grease trap.

Local factors

Where hydro jetting matters most in Torrance

This is directly related to why root intrusion is such a common Torrance call in the first place: the clay and cast-iron sewer laterals typical of Old Torrance and Walteria's earlier construction have joints that shift slightly over decades, giving nearby tree roots an entry point that newer PVC lines, with fewer and tighter joints, don't offer nearly as readily. Once a root has worked its way inside, snaking alone knocks a channel through it but leaves the root mass and the joint gap that let it in, which is exactly why the same line tends to clog again on a predictable schedule until it's actually jetted clear.

For Torrance's commercial kitchens and food-service businesses, grease that escapes a trap gradually coats the inside of the line regardless of pipe age or material, which is why many commercial accounts move to a scheduled jetting maintenance plan rather than waiting for a full backup.

Torrance has a dense food-service economy, including the headquarters of the Japanese grocery chains Mitsuwa Marketplace and Nijiya Market, and the commercial corridors along 190th Street and around the Del Amo area carry many of those businesses. Kitchen grease lines in buildings like these are the classic hydro-jetting job.

How we diagnose it

How a hydro jetting job works

Camera inspection first

We confirm what's actually inside the line, roots, grease, or scale, before jetting, since the approach and equipment differ depending on the material being cleared.

Access point setup

The jetting hose is fed through an existing cleanout or access point wherever possible, avoiding new access excavation.

Progressive high-pressure clearing

Water pressure is calibrated to the pipe's material and condition, clearing buildup along the pipe walls rather than just punching through the center.

Camera verification after

A follow-up camera pass confirms the line has been cleared to its full diameter, not just enough to restore flow temporarily.

When a snake isn't enough

"They tried to snake it and it got stuck"

A common story we hear: a snake was used, it got stuck partway through the line or cleared the immediate blockage, and the clog came back within weeks. That pattern usually means the pipe has a buildup problem along its length, not a single blockage, which is exactly the situation jetting is built to address.

The plumber's snake got stuck at about 60 feet. What does that mean?

It often means there's a significant obstruction, commonly root intrusion or a structural issue, at that point in the line. A camera inspection can confirm exactly what's there before we jet or recommend a repair.

How is jetting different from just snaking again?

Snaking creates a channel through a clog. Jetting scours the full interior surface of the pipe, removing the buildup that caused the clog in the first place.

Maintenance jetting

Jetting as prevention, not just a fix

For a property with a known root-intrusion history, jetting on a set schedule, once a year for many residential lines, once a quarter for a busy commercial kitchen, keeps the line clear before it ever backs up rather than waiting for the next emergency call. This is usually far cheaper over time than repeated emergency snaking visits for the same recurring line.

We can set up exactly this kind of scheduled jetting service for a Torrance property with a documented history of root or grease problems, so the maintenance happens on a calendar rather than depending on someone noticing a slow drain first.

Jet or snake

When jetting does a better job than a cable

A cable cuts a channel through a clog. A hydro jet scours the entire pipe wall with high-pressure water, so grease, scale, and sludge come off instead of closing back up. That is why jetting helps lines that keep clogging every couple of years.

If a snake stopped partway, or a previous visit found roots, a camera look comes first. We only recommend jetting when the pipe can take it. For one-time clogs, regular drain cleaning is often enough.

The visit

Camera first, jet second, camera again

A good jetting visit has three steps. The camera shows the condition of the pipe before any pressure is used. The jet then clears the line. A second pass with the camera confirms the pipe is open and shows whether a repair is needed.

Some callers want jetting because of a recurring sewer smell. That can help if buildup is the cause, but a dry trap or cracked pipe produces the same odor, so we check before assuming. If the camera finds damage, see our sewer line repair page.

FAQ

Questions about hydro jetting in Torrance

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

We calibrate pressure to the pipe's material and condition, and a camera inspection beforehand helps us confirm the line can handle jetting safely.

Can hydro jetting damage my pipes?

When pressure is properly calibrated and the line's condition is checked first, jetting is a standard, safe method used across both residential and commercial plumbing.

Do you offer scheduled jetting for commercial kitchens?

Yes, a scheduled maintenance jetting plan is common for restaurant and food-service accounts to stay ahead of grease buildup.

How often should a root-prone line be jetted?

Once a year is typical for a residential line with a known root history. A busier commercial line may benefit from quarterly service instead.

Can I get an estimate first, or can you jet the same day?

Tell the dispatcher what is happening. For an active backup, the goal is to get someone out quickly. For a planned jetting, you can schedule a visit and get a clear price before work starts.

Will jetting damage old pipes?

It can on weak pipe, which is why we run a camera first. Jetting is recommended only when the pipe can handle it.

How often should a line be jetted?

It depends on what causes the buildup. A line that clogs every two or three years may benefit from scheduled jetting, and a camera shows how fast buildup returns.

Does jetting remove tree roots?

It cuts and flushes root growth from the pipe. Roots come back unless the joint they entered is repaired, so a follow-up camera look is worthwhile.
