Lawndale by the numbers

Two square miles and more than 31,000 neighbors

Lawndale is one of the most compact cities in the South Bay. The Census Bureau counts about 31,800 residents on roughly 2 square miles of land, which works out to more than 16,000 people per square mile, and just under 38 percent of homes are owner-occupied. On the ground that means short blocks, shared fences and walls, and a lot of rental property, so a plumbing failure rarely stays one household's private problem.

The city sits directly north of Torrance, along the stretch where Redondo Beach Boulevard marks Torrance's northern edge, and its main spine is Hawthorne Boulevard, which began as the route of the Los Angeles and Redondo Railway when it reached Lawndale in 1902. After World War II, returning veterans and new arrivals turned the area into a bedroom community, so most of the housing dates from that building boom. Homes from that era are now six or seven decades old, which is the age where original supply lines, water heaters, and shutoff valves start failing on their own schedule.

Burst pipes in a dense block

Why speed matters more on a tight lot

When houses and apartment buildings sit close together, escaping water finds a path into a shared wall, a garage, or a neighbor's yard quickly. Shutting off the main valve is always the first step, and if you rent, the owner or manager should hear about it the same day so the repair and any drying can be coordinated.

After the line is repaired we check the surrounding connections, including the shutoff valve that should have stopped the water, since a valve that will not close is a common reason a small leak becomes a large one.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Lawndale

In a city this densely built, a burst pipe can reach a neighbor's wall as quickly as your own. Here is what to do first, before help arrives.

Shut off the main valve

Stop the water at the source, if you can safely reach the main shutoff.

Kill power to wet areas

If water has reached an outlet or panel, cut power to that area.

Clear the immediate area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the water.

Call our 24/7 line

Local FAQ

Questions from Lawndale residents

Do you work with landlords and property managers in Lawndale?

Yes. A large share of Lawndale homes are rentals, so we coordinate access and scheduling directly with owners and managers and provide written documentation of what was found and repaired.

Is Lawndale's water the same as Torrance's?

Not the same utility. Lawndale is served by Golden State Water Company, while most of Torrance is served by Torrance Municipal Water. Regional hardness is similar, so scale buildup affects Lawndale homes much as it does Torrance homes.

How do I know if my Lawndale home needs a repipe instead of another repair?

Repeated leaks in different spots, low pressure at every fixture, and discolored water from several taps are the usual signs. A pressure test and a look at the pipe material tell us whether a targeted repair or a repipe makes more sense.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Lawndale?

Yes, we cover both Lawndale ZIP codes, 90260 and 90261, along with the neighboring Torrance, Redondo Beach, Hawthorne, and Gardena ZIP codes.

Is emergency service available in Lawndale?

Yes, our 24/7 emergency line covers Lawndale the same as the rest of our service area, including nights, weekends, and holidays for burst pipes and active leaks.
