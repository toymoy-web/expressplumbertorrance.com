Local factors

Why backflow prevention matters here

A backflow preventer stops contaminated water, from an irrigation system, a pool, or a commercial process, from being pulled backward into the drinking water supply during a pressure drop. Torrance Municipal Water and other California utilities generally require annual testing on these devices for properties that have one installed, most commonly irrigation systems and certain commercial connections.

Because this is a compliance requirement, not just a maintenance suggestion, missing a test cycle can result in a notice from the utility or, in some cases, a service restriction until the device is tested and certified.

A large share of backflow testing in Torrance comes from commercial and irrigated properties, such as the office and retail concentration along 190th Street between Hawthorne Boulevard and Western Avenue near the Del Amo Fashion Center. Residential lots with irrigation systems, pools, or fire sprinklers can fall under the same annual requirement.

How we diagnose it

What a backflow test and service visit includes

Device inspection

We check the physical condition of the backflow preventer assembly before testing.

Pressure differential testing

Certified test equipment checks whether each check valve inside the device is holding pressure correctly.

Repair if needed

If a component fails, we repair or replace it, then re-test before certifying the device.

Documentation and filing

We provide the completed test report, and can file it with the water utility on your behalf where that service is offered.

Not sure what's required

"I got a notice about backflow testing and don't know what to do"

Most homeowners' first contact with backflow testing is a notice from the water utility, not something they went looking for. You don't need to understand the code requirement in detail, just get the device tested by the deadline.

I got a letter saying my backflow device needs testing. What do I do?

Call us with the notice details and we'll schedule the test, handle any repairs the device needs, and provide the documentation the notice requires.

My irrigation system doesn't have a backflow device. Do I need one?

Most irrigation systems are required to have one under current code. We can inspect your system and install one if it's missing.

For property managers

Handling backflow testing across multiple properties

Property managers and landlords with several Torrance properties often find it easier to consolidate backflow testing on a single annual schedule rather than reacting to each utility notice separately as it arrives. We can coordinate testing across multiple addresses and keep documentation organized for each one.

For a commercial property with a fire suppression system, that assembly typically has its own backflow prevention and testing requirement, separate from any irrigation-related device on the same property, and we test both where applicable.

Backflow devices also come in a few distinct types, double-check valve assemblies and reduced-pressure principle assemblies being the most common in residential and light-commercial settings, and the correct type depends on the specific hazard level of what's connected downstream. We confirm the right device type is already installed, or recommend a change if it isn't, rather than simply testing whatever's there.

Notices and deadlines

A water company notice, an annual test, or locked valves

Many callers contact us because the water company sent a notice. Backflow devices usually need a test once a year, and a missed deadline can lead to a shutoff. Keep the notice handy and read us the device location and size when you call.

A device that fails its test may need new parts or a full replacement. We tell you what the tester finds and what the repair involves. For homes with irrigation or other connections, mention that so the technician knows which assembly to expect.

FAQ

Questions about backflow testing in Torrance

How often does a backflow preventer need to be tested?

Most California water utilities, including here in Torrance, require annual testing for properties with a backflow device installed.

What happens if my device fails the test?

We'll repair or replace the failed component and re-test before certifying the device, all typically in the same visit.

Do you handle backflow testing for commercial properties?

Yes, including irrigation and fire-suppression-related backflow devices on commercial properties.

How do I know if I have the right type of backflow device installed?

We check this as part of the inspection, since the required device type depends on the specific hazard level of what's connected, and can recommend a change if the wrong type is in place.

I got a notice from my water company about backflow testing. What do I do?

Call us with the notice in hand. We schedule the test and explain what the result means. Ask the dispatcher how the report reaches the water company.

How often is a backflow assembly tested?

Most devices are tested annually, though your water provider sets the rule for your address. Check the notice for the deadline.

What happens if my device fails the test?

The tester explains the cause. Some devices need a repair kit, and others need to be replaced. We quote the work before starting.

Can you replace a backflow preventer?

Yes. Replacement is quoted after the device is inspected and we know its size and location.
