Manhattan Beach, rebuilt

A city of remodels and rebuilds

Manhattan Beach has about 35,500 residents, was incorporated in 1912, and was built on what used to be sand dunes. Sand Dune Park, off Rosecrans Boulevard, preserves the last exposed dune. Residents divide the city into well-known neighborhoods: the Sand Section along the ocean, with its walk streets and dense condo and multi-family blocks, the Hill Section, where many homes have been remodeled or newly built, the Tree Section, El Porto at the north end, and Manhattan Village and Liberty Village on the east side.

The 90266 ZIP code has appeared in published rankings among the most expensive in the country, and that shows up in the work. When a house is remodeled or rebuilt, its plumbing is usually reworked along with it. New water heaters, relocated gas lines, and repiped walls are routine parts of those projects, and we coordinate with the general contractor so the plumbing rough-in and inspections land at the right point in the schedule.

What to expect from a Manhattan Beach call

Manhattan Beach lies just north of Hermosa Beach and Redondo Beach, a short drive up the coast from our Torrance base. Landmarks such as the Manhattan Beach Pier and its Roundhouse Aquarium, and Polliwog Park in the Hill Section, are useful reference points when a customer is describing where a problem is.

Coverage here reaches the neighboring ZIP codes too, including Hermosa Beach (90254), El Segundo (90245), Redondo Beach (90278), and Lawndale (90260), for water heater installation, leak detection, and repiping.

Manhattan Beach is served by its own city water system rather than by Cal Water or Golden State Water. Water hardness across the South Bay is similar, so scale buildup inside water heaters is a concern here just as it is in Torrance.

Pricing is confirmed before work starts and is based on what the technician finds, with no coastal surcharge for the address.

Sand, soil, and older beach cottages

What the ground and the housing mix mean for plumbing

Because the land was originally dune sand, underground work here is usually easy to excavate, but older beach cottages often still run original lines that predate the remodel boom around them. When a cottage is kept and added onto, we inspect the old supply and drain lines before the new work ties into them, since connecting new plumbing to a failing old line just moves the leak.

Garage-mounted water heaters close to the coast are worth a yearly look at the connections and the relief valve discharge, because salt air is hardest on exactly those exposed parts.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Manhattan Beach

A beach-city home sits close to the sand, and a failed supply line or water heater can do real damage fast. Here is what to do right away.

Shut off the main valve

Stop the water at the source, if you can safely reach the main shutoff.

Kill power to wet areas

If water has reached an outlet or panel, cut power to that area.

Clear the immediate area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the water.

Local FAQ

Questions from Manhattan Beach residents

Do you handle remodel and rebuild plumbing in Manhattan Beach?

Yes. We coordinate with your general contractor on rough-in timing, permits, and inspections, and we handle water heater, gas line, and repiping work as part of the project schedule.

Does salt air really affect plumbing near the Strand?

It does, mainly on exposed parts such as hose bibs, garage water heater connections, and outdoor fittings. Lines sealed inside walls are much less affected.

Who supplies water in Manhattan Beach?

The city runs its own municipal water system rather than buying service from a private utility. Regional hardness is similar to Torrance, so the same scale buildup applies to water heaters.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Manhattan Beach?

Yes. The city uses ZIP code 90266, and 90267 is for PO boxes only. We also cover the neighboring Hermosa Beach, El Segundo, Redondo Beach, and Lawndale ZIP codes.

Is emergency service available in Manhattan Beach?

Yes, our 24/7 emergency line covers Manhattan Beach the same as the rest of our service area, including nights, weekends, and holidays for burst pipes and failed water heaters.
