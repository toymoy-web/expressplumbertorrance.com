Signs

Signs you may have a slab leak

Warm spot on the floor

A patch of flooring that's noticeably warmer than the rest of the room, especially near the water heater, often means a hot-water line is leaking under the slab.

Water coming up under floorboards

Moisture or standing water appearing at the base of flooring, particularly in older homes, is a common way a slab leak first becomes visible.

Unexplained water bill increase

A slab leak can run for weeks before it's visible, and a climbing water bill is often the first real clue something is wrong underneath the floor.

Cracks in flooring or walls

Sustained water under a slab can shift the foundation slightly, showing up as new cracks in tile, drywall, or the slab itself.

Local factors

Not every Torrance home has the same foundation

West Torrance's post-war tract homes, built through the 1950s and 60s, were constructed on a mix of foundation types: some tracts sit on slab-on-grade, while others use a raised pier-and-beam foundation with a crawlspace underneath. That distinction matters directly for a leak repair, because a pier-and-beam home often allows us to access and repair a leaking line from the crawlspace without cutting into a floor at all.

Older Old Torrance bungalows are more likely to have a raised foundation as well, while true slab-on-grade construction, where the plumbing runs directly under a poured concrete floor, is more common in some of the later West Torrance tracts. We confirm which type your home has before proposing a repair method, rather than assuming based on the home's age alone.

How we diagnose it

How we confirm a slab leak before repair

Pressure and meter test

We confirm water loss is actually occurring in the system before assuming the cause is a slab leak specifically.

Electronic and acoustic tracing

We track the specific frequency pressurized water makes escaping under concrete, narrowing the search area to a few feet before anything is opened.

Thermal imaging

For hot-water line leaks, thermal imaging can help confirm the warm spot's exact boundaries before any cutting begins.

Access point confirmation

For pier-and-beam homes, we check whether crawlspace access can reach the leak before considering a slab penetration at all.

Not sure what's actually wrong

"There's a warm spot on my floor and I don't know why"

Most slab leak calls in Torrance start with a symptom, not a diagnosis: a warm patch on the floor, water showing up under the baseboards, or a driveway that stays damp longer than it should after watering the yard. You don't need to already know it's a slab leak, that's exactly what detection is for.

I have a warm spot on the floor near the garage. What does that mean?

A warm patch, especially near where a water heater or hot-water line runs, is one of the most common ways a slab leak shows up. It's worth having checked before the leak grows.

There's water coming up under my floorboards. Is that a slab leak?

It can be, especially in an older home. We'll confirm with electronic detection before recommending any repair, so you're not guessing at the cause.

My driveway stays wet longer than the rest of the yard. Could that be a leak?

Yes, a leak under a concrete driveway or slab edge is a real possibility, particularly if the dampness appears without a clear source like sprinklers.

Repair options

How a slab leak actually gets fixed

Once the leak is located, the repair method depends on the home's foundation and the leak's exact location. In a pier-and-beam home, we can often reroute or repair the line from the crawlspace, avoiding the slab entirely. In a true slab-on-grade home, repair may mean a targeted concrete cut directly over the leak, or in some cases rerouting the line overhead through the attic instead of cutting the floor at all.

We'll walk through the realistic options for your specific home and leak location, including the trade-offs of a spot repair versus a full reroute, before any work begins.

FAQ

Questions about slab leak repair in Torrance

Do you have to cut my concrete floor to fix a slab leak?

Not always. If your home has a raised, pier-and-beam foundation, we may be able to access and repair the line from the crawlspace instead.

How do you find a slab leak without seeing it?

We use electronic and acoustic leak-tracing equipment, sometimes paired with thermal imaging, to pinpoint the leak's location before any concrete is opened.

Will a slab leak damage my foundation if I wait?

It can. Sustained water under a slab can lead to soil movement and cracking over time, which is why we recommend addressing a confirmed leak promptly.
