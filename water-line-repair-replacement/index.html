Signs

Signs of a main water line problem

Low pressure at every fixture

When pressure drops throughout the whole house rather than at one fixture, the main line between the meter and the house is a likely cause.

Wet spot in the yard between the meter and house

A consistently damp or unusually green patch of lawn along the likely path of the service line often means it's leaking underground.

Water bill increase with no indoor leak found

If indoor leak detection comes back clear but the bill keeps climbing, the leak may be on the underground line itself.

Discolored water after the meter

Sediment or discoloration that starts right at the meter, rather than developing further into the house, can point to a failing service line.

A broken main water line can flood a yard or crawlspace quickly and drive up your water bill by the hour. Close the main shutoff, then call our 24/7 emergency line, and a technician can be dispatched any hour to confirm the break and plan a repair or trenchless replacement.

Local factors

What affects a Torrance water line's condition

The service line between Torrance Municipal Water's meter and your home is typically the homeowner's responsibility to repair, and its material and age often mirror the home's interior plumbing. Older galvanized or early copper service lines in Old Torrance and Walteria are more prone to corrosion-related failure than the more corrosion-resistant materials used in newer installations.

Naturally hard Los Angeles County water also plays a role here just as it does inside the home, gradually narrowing an aging line's effective diameter even before an outright leak develops, which is part of why a whole-house pressure drop is worth investigating rather than assuming it's just the water utility's supply pressure.

North Torrance, around the Crenshaw Boulevard corridor, and Walteria in the south sit at opposite ends of the city, but in both the pipe from the meter to the house is typically the homeowner's responsibility. Trenchless replacement is often the better fit on lots with mature trees and finished driveways.

How we diagnose it

How we diagnose a water line issue

Pressure test at the meter

We confirm whether incoming pressure at the meter matches what's reaching your fixtures, isolating whether the problem is the line itself.

Leak tracing along the line's path

Electronic and acoustic tracing follows the likely path of the service line to pinpoint a leak's location.

Camera inspection where accessible

For older lines, a camera can sometimes confirm internal corrosion or material failure directly.

Repair method recommendation

Depending on what we find, we'll recommend a spot repair, trenchless replacement, or full open-trench replacement, with the trade-offs of each explained.

Not sure what's actually wrong

"We suspect it's from the meter to the house"

Homeowners often narrow this down themselves before calling: everything indoors checks out, but pressure is still low or the meter is still moving. That's exactly the situation a water line inspection is built for.

We shut everything off and the meter still runs. Does that mean it's the main line?

It's one of the most likely explanations, yes, since it suggests water is being lost somewhere between the meter and your home's interior shutoff.

Do you need to dig up my whole yard to fix a water line?

Not always. Trenchless replacement can often install a new line with only small access pits at each end, depending on the yard's layout and the line's condition.

Trenchless vs. open trench

Why trenchless isn't always possible, but often is

Trenchless water line replacement pulls a new line through the ground using the old line's path or a nearby bore, avoiding a long open trench across your yard, driveway, or landscaping. It works well for most straight-line residential runs, though a service line with tight bends, unusual depth, or interference from other utilities may still require a more traditional open-trench approach.

We'll assess your specific yard and line path before recommending either method, since the goal is picking what actually works for your property, not defaulting to whichever is more profitable to sell.

Warning signs

Signs the line from the meter to the house is failing

Meter spins with the house shut off

If the dial keeps moving after you close the house main, the loss is likely on the buried line before the shutoff.

A wet patch or green strip over the line

Water rising from a break can soften soil and feed grass along the pipe route.

Low pressure or no water

A cracked or crushed line can starve the house of flow even when the meter is open.

A climbing water bill

A buried leak adds usage with no visible cause. Bills creep up before anything shows at the surface.

In most systems the utility is responsible up to the meter, and the homeowner is responsible from the meter to the house. Check your address with Torrance Municipal Water or Cal Water, then call us for the private side.

Repair options

Spot repair, partial replacement, or the whole line

If the line is sound except for one failed joint, a spot repair is enough. When the pipe is corroded, galvanized, or has failed in more than one place, replacing the full length is more reliable. Trenchless methods can reduce digging in the right conditions.

Callers sometimes ask about low pressure alone. It can come from a partly closed valve, a failing pressure regulator, or a leak, so we check those before proposing a replacement. If you only have no water at all and need help right away, call the emergency line. For hidden leaks, our leak detection page shows how we confirm the problem first.

FAQ

Questions about water line repair in Torrance

Who is responsible for the water line, me or the utility?

Generally, the property owner is responsible for the service line from the meter to the house, while the utility maintains the main in the street. We can help clarify which section a problem falls in.

How long does trenchless water line replacement take?

Most trenchless replacements are completed in a single day, depending on the line's length and site access.

Will replacing my water line disrupt my landscaping?

Trenchless methods generally limit disruption to small access pits rather than a full trench, though final impact depends on your yard's specific layout.

How do I know the leak is on my side of the meter?

Shut off the house main and watch the meter. If it stops, the loss is inside the property. If it keeps moving, the leak is before the shutoff, and the buried line is the likely place to look.

Can you replace the water line without trenching the whole yard?

Sometimes. Trenchless methods work in the right soil and pipe conditions, so the technician checks the route and tells you what is realistic.

What should I do if I have no water at all?

First check whether the utility has an outage or shutoff on your account. If not, call us and we will look at the main valve, the line, and the pressure regulator.

Is low pressure a sign of a leaking water line?

It can be. A leak, a partly closed valve, or a failing regulator can all cause it. A pressure test separates them.

What does a main water line replacement involve?

The old line is exposed along its route, the new pipe is installed to the house connection, and the system is tested. Scope and price depend on length, depth, and what sits above the line.
