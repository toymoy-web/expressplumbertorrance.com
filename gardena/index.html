Gardena's neighborhoods

From Gardena Heights to the Willows

Gardena is one of the more densely built cities bordering Torrance, spanning three ZIP codes (90247, 90248, 90249) with a real mix of single-family blocks in areas like Gardena Heights and denser multi-family construction nearer the city's commercial corridors. On the city's east side sits the Gardena Willows Wetland Preserve, a 13.6-acre remnant of the historic Dominguez Slough, a marsh system that once covered a much larger stretch of this part of the South Bay before it was almost entirely paved over.

Built over an old slough: why drainage matters here

Why sump pump and drainage calls run higher in Gardena

Historic marshland underfoot

The Dominguez Slough once covered as much as 400 acres of this area. The Willows preserve is the only piece left undeveloped, but the underlying ground conditions haven't disappeared just because the marsh was paved over.

Dense construction, less permeable ground

Gardena's higher building density means less open ground to absorb heavy rain compared to lower-density parts of Torrance, pushing more water into the storm and sump systems that are in place.

Older multi-family properties

Basement and below-grade storage areas in some of Gardena's older multi-family buildings are exactly the kind of space a sump pump is meant to protect.

Emergency dispatch around the clock

A failed sump pump during a storm is treated as the emergency it is, with dispatch available in Gardena any hour of the day or night.

Working with Gardena's building stock

What a sump pump or drainage call looks like here

On a Gardena sump pump call, we start by confirming the discharge path actually clears the property rather than just moving water a few feet before it pools again, a real issue on some of the denser multi-family lots near the city's commercial corridors. For a property manager overseeing several older buildings, we can also set up a pre-storm-season check across multiple addresses rather than waiting for the first unit to fail during an actual storm.

Response times in Gardena match our core Torrance coverage, and a technician arriving for a sump pump or drainage call typically carries the same equipment used on our Torrance calls near the Madrona Marsh area, since the underlying concern, historic low-lying ground, is similar in both places.

This coverage extends into the surrounding ZIP codes too, including Lawndale (90260), Hawthorne (90250), and Carson (90745), for sump pump, sewer line, and water heater work.

The Willows preserve itself is a useful, physical reminder of what the wider watershed used to do naturally, absorb and slow down rainfall before it reached open water. With 96% of that watershed now built over, the 13.6 acres the preserve protects are the exception, not the rule, and the rest of Gardena's rainfall depends on engineered systems doing the job the marsh once did.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Gardena

Gardena's dense construction and drainage history mean water can back up fast during a storm. Here is what to do the moment you spot rising water.

Shut off the main valve

Stop the water at the source first, if you can safely reach the main shutoff valve.

Kill power to wet areas

If water has reached an outlet or panel, cut power to that area rather than working around it.

Clear the immediate area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the water while help is on the way.

Call our 24/7 line

A live dispatcher answers any hour and can walk you through the next step while a technician heads your way.

Local FAQ

Questions from Gardena residents

Does the old Dominguez Slough actually affect my plumbing today?

Indirectly, yes. The area's history as marshland means drainage infrastructure carries a heavier load during storms than a city built on naturally well-drained ground, which is part of why we pay closer attention to sump pump and drainage calls in Gardena.

Is Gardena's water the same utility as Torrance's?

No, Gardena is served by Golden State Water Company rather than Torrance Municipal Water or Cal Water, though the regional water hardness is similar.

Can you service a multi-family building, not just a single-family home?

Yes, we regularly work with owners and managers of Gardena's denser multi-family properties, including basement and below-grade drainage systems.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Gardena?

Yes, we cover all three Gardena ZIP codes, 90247, 90248, and 90249, plus the immediately surrounding Lawndale, Hawthorne, and Carson ZIP codes.

Is emergency service available in Gardena?

Yes, our 24/7 emergency line covers Gardena the same as the rest of our service area, including sump pump failures and active leaks during storms.
