Rancho Palos Verdes neighborhoods

The Peninsula's largest city, one neighborhood at a time

Rancho Palos Verdes is the biggest of the four Palos Verdes Peninsula cities, with about 42,300 residents, and it was incorporated in 1973. It runs along the bluffs from Miraleste in the east, past Seaview and Portuguese Bend, to Abalone Cove and the coast. Landmarks include the Point Vicente Lighthouse, built in 1926 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places, Lloyd Wright's Wayfarers Chapel, and the Terranea Resort, one of the city's largest employers.

Why leak detection leads here

Water lines and moving ground

Part of the city sits on the Greater Portuguese Bend landslide complex. A slide that began in 1956 destroyed about 140 homes in the Portuguese Bend area, and the ground there is still moving. The city geologist has said movement now averages one to three feet per year and that rainfall is the primary driver. California declared a state of emergency over the Rancho Palos Verdes landslide in 2024, after movement forced power and gas shutoffs to dozens of homes. Residents have also pointed to underground waterline breaks as a contributing factor, and Cal Water has confirmed water main leaks caused by land movement elsewhere on the Peninsula.

A plumber cannot stabilize a hillside, and we do not offer engineering opinions on land movement. What we can do is find hidden leaks in the water and sewer lines on your property, repair them, and document the work, so that a failing line is not adding water to ground that is already under stress.

Miraleste and the eastern bluffs

Miraleste was part of the original 1920s Palos Verdes Project, so some original supply and sewer lines here have had many decades of service.

Seaview and Portuguese Bend

These neighborhoods sit closest to the active landslide area, where utility shutoffs have affected power and gas service and water line breaks are a recurring concern.

Coastal exposure at Abalone Cove

Ocean-facing properties see salt air and marine humidity that corrode exposed fittings faster than inland homes.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Rancho Palos Verdes

On a hillside, escaping water can spread through soil as well as through a house. These are the first steps while a technician is on the way.

Shut off the main valve

Stop the water at the source, if you can safely reach the main shutoff.

Kill power to wet areas

If water has reached an outlet or panel, cut power to that area.

Clear the immediate area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the water.

Call our 24/7 line

Questions from Rancho Palos Verdes residents

Can you find a leak on a lot with active ground movement?

Yes. Electronic and acoustic tracing works regardless of the terrain, though movement can shift pipes over time. We document what we find and recommend you share it with your geologist or engineer.

Is the Portuguese Bend area part of your coverage?

Yes, we serve Portuguese Bend, Seaview, Miraleste, and Abalone Cove. Where the city has red-tagged a home or shut off utilities, we follow its restrictions and coordinate before any work starts.

Who supplies water in Rancho Palos Verdes?
