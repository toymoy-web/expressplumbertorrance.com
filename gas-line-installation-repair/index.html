Signs

Signs of a gas line problem

Smell of gas near an appliance or line

A rotten-egg smell near a gas appliance or along a visible line is a genuine emergency and should be treated as one immediately.

Hissing sound near a gas connection

An audible hissing at a fitting or valve can indicate an active leak, even without a strong odor yet.

Appliance not getting enough gas

A stove burner or furnace that won't reach full output can point to a sizing or pressure issue in the line feeding it, not just the appliance itself.

Adding a new gas appliance

Installing a new range, dryer, or outdoor fire feature usually requires extending or resizing the existing gas line to handle the added load.

Yellow or flickering pilot flame

A gas flame that burns yellow or flickers rather than steady blue can indicate a combustion or supply issue worth having checked, separate from an outright leak.

If you smell gas, treat it as an emergency. Leave the home, avoid switches and flames, and call our 24/7 emergency line from outside. A licensed technician is available any hour to find and repair the leak, then pressure test the line before service is restored.

Local factors

What matters for gas line work in Torrance

Gas line work in Torrance requires a permit and inspection like most California jurisdictions, and line sizing has to account for the total gas load of everything connected to it, not just the single new appliance being added. Undersizing a line when adding an appliance is a common cause of underperforming appliances even when the appliance itself is functioning correctly.

Older Torrance homes may still have galvanized gas piping in sections, which is more prone to internal corrosion restricting flow over time than the black iron or corrugated stainless steel tubing used in newer installations.

Remodels in Southwood and Hollywood Riviera that add a range, a fireplace, or a new water heater often need a gas line extended or resized. Any new or modified gas line needs a permit and a pressure test, and we schedule the inspection so the gas stays off only as long as necessary.

How we diagnose it

How we handle gas line work

Leak detection first

For a suspected leak, we prioritize locating and isolating it safely before any other diagnostic work.

Load calculation for new appliances

We calculate the total gas demand of everything on the line, including the new appliance, to confirm the line is sized correctly.

Pressure testing

New and repaired lines are pressure tested to confirm there are no leaks before the line is put back into service.

Permit and inspection coordination

We pull the required permit and coordinate inspection as part of the job, not as an afterthought.

Safety first

What to do if you smell gas

If you smell gas, do not operate light switches, appliances, or anything that could create a spark. Leave the area, and if you can safely do so from outside, shut off the gas at the meter. Call us or your gas utility's emergency line immediately from a safe location.

Once the immediate safety issue is addressed, we can locate the source of the leak and complete whatever repair is needed, along with the pressure test required before the line goes back into service.

For a new appliance installation rather than an emergency, we'll also check whether your home's gas meter and regulator can support the added load alongside everything already connected, since the meter itself, not just the interior piping, sometimes needs to be upsized by the utility.

Inspection

Gas line checks, pressure tests, and leak reports

Some callers want a gas line checked, not repaired. That might be before a renovation, after a rusty section is noticed, or when buying a home. A pressure test tells us whether the line holds, and a technician inspects fittings, connections, and shutoffs.

If you need a written report, ask the dispatcher what documentation is available before booking. If you smell gas right now, leave the home and call from outside.

New hookups

Stoves, barbecues, and fireplace supply lines

New appliances need a properly sized gas line, a shutoff, and a tested connection. A stove or dryer hookup is a routine job. A barbecue or outdoor line needs a route and a shutoff location planned first.

Fireplace inserts need gas supplied to them correctly, while the appliance itself may need its own service. Tell us the brand and fuel type. If the same project involves a new water heater, our replacement page explains how gas lines are handled there.

FAQ

Questions about gas line service in Torrance

Do you handle gas leak emergencies?

Yes, a suspected gas leak is treated as an emergency call, available 24/7.

Do I need a permit to add a gas line for a new appliance?

In most cases, yes, in Torrance and surrounding cities. We handle pulling the permit as part of the installation.

Can you upsize an existing gas line for a new appliance?

Yes, we calculate the total load and resize the line as needed to safely support the new appliance alongside existing ones.

Will my gas meter need to be upgraded for a new appliance?

Sometimes. If the added load exceeds what your current meter and regulator can support, that upgrade goes through the gas utility, and we can help identify whether it's needed before work starts.

Can you inspect for a gas leak and give me a report?

We can inspect and pressure test the line. Ask the dispatcher what written documentation is available for your situation before booking.

Can you connect a new gas stove or barbecue?

Yes. Tell us the appliance and where it will sit. The technician plans the line, shutoff, and test.

Do you convert appliances between natural gas and propane?

That is a manufacturer-specific job and it is not something we list. We handle the gas supply line to the appliance.

A section of my gas line looks rusty. Should I be worried?

A technician should look at it. Surface rust may be minor, but a pressure test and inspection give a real answer.
