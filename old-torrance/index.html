About Old Torrance

A planned town from 1912, still on its original streets

Old Torrance is where the city began. Torrance was formed in 1912 and formally incorporated in 1921. The founders hired the Olmsted landscape architecture firm to plan the town, and much of that plan is still in place. Many of the downtown streets marked their 100th anniversary in 2012.

The architect Irving Gill designed several early homes and civic buildings here, including the Pacific Electric Railway bridge at El Prado in 1913. The Torrance Historical Society has offered a free, docent-led walking tour of the area from the Torrance Historical Museum at 1345 Post Avenue. Our service area page places Old Torrance roughly between Madrona Avenue and Western Avenue, from 190th Street south to Sepulveda Boulevard. Residential streets such as Gramercy Avenue sit in ZIP code 90501.

Plumbing here

What older, mixed-age streets mean for plumbing

A neighborhood this old has been remodeled many times. One house may have new supply lines while the house next door does not, so we treat each address on its own.

Start with what is there

Pipe materials vary from house to house on streets this old. We look at the actual lines before recommending a repair or a replacement.

Camera before you commit

A sewer line is hidden. A drain camera shows its condition on the day we run it, which helps before a purchase or a remodel.

Mature street trees

Torrance has more than 90,000 trees, and large trees are common on older blocks. Roots can enter a sewer line anywhere, and only a camera shows whether they have.

Permits for remodels

The City of Torrance handles building permits inside the city. Ask us on the call whether your job is likely to need one.

What we do not assume

Age does not tell us the pipes

We do not assume that a house's age tells us what is inside its walls. Some Old Torrance houses have had supply lines or sewer laterals replaced and some have not. A pressure test, a leak survey or a camera run gives a real answer, and a build date does not.

Torrance Municipal Water serves most of the city, and California Water Service serves some western blocks, so the utility depends on the address. Your water bill names yours.

Local FAQ

Questions from Old Torrance residents

Do you serve all of Old Torrance, including ZIP 90501?

Yes. We cover Old Torrance and the rest of the city, ZIP codes 90501 through 90505. Call and give your street address.

Is a camera inspection worth doing before buying an older Old Torrance house?

It is often a sensible step because a sewer line is hidden. A camera shows its condition at that moment, including cracks, offsets and roots. It cannot predict what will happen later.

Does an older house need to be repiped?

Not automatically. Age alone does not decide it. Repeated leaks, low pressure at every fixture or discolored water from several taps are the signs worth investigating, and a pressure test tells us more than a build date.

Who issues permits for plumbing work in Old Torrance?

The City of Torrance handles building permits for properties inside the city. Ask us on the call whether your job needs one.
