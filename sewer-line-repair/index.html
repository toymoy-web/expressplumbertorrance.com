Signs

Signs of a sewer line problem

Multiple slow drains at once

When several fixtures throughout the house drain slowly at the same time, the problem is usually in the main sewer line, not an individual fixture.

Sewage smell in the yard or house

A persistent sewage odor, especially near the yard's cleanout or a floor drain, can mean a cracked or blocked sewer line.

Sewage backing up into the lowest drain

A backup into a tub, shower, or floor drain when you run the washing machine or flush a toilet is a classic sign of a main line blockage.

Unusually green or soggy patch of lawn

A lush or soggy patch of yard, especially in dry weather, can mean a sewer line underneath it is leaking.

A sewage backup in a tub, floor drain, or toilet is an emergency, because wastewater can spread into flooring and drywall within hours. Call our 24/7 emergency line, stop using water in the home, and a technician can be dispatched any hour of the day or night to find the blockage with a camera.

Local factors

Why root intrusion is common in older Torrance neighborhoods

Old Torrance and parts of Walteria have mature street trees planted decades ago alongside clay or cast-iron sewer laterals that were standard for that era of construction. Tree roots are drawn to the moisture and nutrients inside a sewer line and can work their way into small cracks or joints, gradually restricting flow until a full backup occurs.

Newer construction in North Torrance is more likely to use PVC laterals, which resist root intrusion far better than older materials, though PVC lines can still develop problems at joints or from ground settling over time.

The original Old Torrance tract and the streets around it are the oldest part of the city, and the central district is bounded by Madrona Avenue, 190th Street, Western Avenue, and Sepulveda Boulevard. For a buyer or remodeler anywhere in that area, a camera inspection of the sewer lateral before work begins costs far less than a surprise failure afterward.

How we diagnose it

How we diagnose a sewer line problem

Initial clearing

For an active backup, we first clear the immediate blockage so the line is flowing again.

Camera inspection

A sewer camera confirms the actual cause: root intrusion, a cracked pipe, a low spot (belly) in the line, or a full collapse.

Locate the problem area

Camera footage is paired with a locating tool to mark the exact spot of the issue from above ground.

Repair method recommendation

Based on what the camera shows, we recommend a spot repair, trenchless lining, or full replacement, and explain why.

A common real case

"A localized crack with root intrusion, looking for a spot repair"

Not every sewer line problem requires replacing the whole line. When a camera inspection shows a single, localized crack with root intrusion rather than widespread deterioration, a targeted spot repair or trenchless lining at that one location is often enough, and considerably less disruptive than a full excavation.

The camera found one cracked spot with roots. Do I need the whole line replaced?

Not necessarily. If the rest of the line is in reasonably good condition, a spot repair or trenchless lining at the damaged section is often the more practical fix.

What's the difference between a spot repair and trenchless lining?

A spot repair addresses one specific section of pipe, usually with limited excavation. Trenchless lining installs a new pipe liner inside the existing line, often without digging a trench at all, and can address a longer damaged section.

Property owners

Sewer line work on rental and commercial property

A recurring backup at a rental property or small commercial building is worth diagnosing properly rather than repeatedly snaking the same symptom. When a landlord or property manager needs the work logged for a tenant file or an insurance claim, we handle scheduling and access directly with them and provide clear documentation of what was found and fixed.

For a commercial kitchen or multi-unit building, grease and higher line volume can accelerate the same root-intrusion and scale problems seen on residential lines, sometimes on a faster timeline. A camera inspection is just as useful there for deciding between a maintenance jetting schedule and an actual repair.

Backup warning signs

Sewage coming up in the house: what to do first

A sewer backup rarely starts with a flood. It starts with a toilet that bubbles, two fixtures that drain slowly together, or water that appears in a floor drain. Each one is a signal that the line is blocked or damaged below the house.

Stop adding water

Pause the washer, dishwasher, and showers. Every gallon you send down has nowhere to go.

Keep people and pets away

Wastewater carries bacteria. Keep children out of the area and avoid touching wet flooring with bare hands.

Do not use chemical openers

They rarely clear a main line and they make the work harsher for the technician who follows.

Note when it started

Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are affected and which drain filled first. That points us to the right section of pipe.

The repair decision

Spot repair, trenchless, or full replacement

After the camera inspection, the choice follows what the pipe shows. A single crack with some root growth can often be fixed locally. A long stretch with sags, offsets, or repeated collapse is usually better replaced.

Trenchless options are available when site conditions allow, so you may be able to avoid trenching your whole yard. A line that only clogs now and then may simply need drain cleaning or jetting first. Our leak detection team can help if you suspect the line is also leaking water into the soil.

FAQ

Questions about sewer line repair in Torrance

Do you offer trenchless sewer repair?

Yes, trenchless lining is an option for many sewer line repairs, depending on what the camera inspection shows.

Will you show me the camera footage?

Yes, we can walk you through the footage so you understand exactly what's wrong before agreeing to a repair method.

Do you handle sewer line work for small commercial properties?

Yes, including camera inspection and repair for small Torrance businesses and commercial buildings.

Can you work directly with my property management company?

Yes, we're used to being the point of contact for a management company rather than the tenant, including access arrangements and after-the-fact reporting.

How can you tell tree roots are in my sewer line?

A camera shows fine roots threading through joints or a dense mat blocking the pipe. Older clay and cast-iron laterals in the older parts of Torrance are especially prone to it.

Can a cracked section be fixed without replacing the whole sewer line?

Often, yes. A localized crack or offset can be repaired at that spot once the camera shows the rest of the line is sound. If the damage is spread out, replacement makes more sense.

Why does my bathroom smell like sewage when the drains work?

A dry trap, a failed seal, or a cracked drain pipe can let sewer gas into the room. A trap that stopped holding water, or a slow leak under the fixture, is a common cause.

Will a trenchless repair work on my line?

It depends on depth, pipe condition, and what sits above the line. The technician reviews the camera footage first and tells you whether trenchless work is realistic for your property.

How long does a clog come back after I clear it?

If it returns every couple of years, roots or a damaged section are usually the reason. A camera look tells you whether cleaning is enough or a repair is overdue.
