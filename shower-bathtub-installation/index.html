Signs

When it's time for a shower or tub update

Old valve can't be repaired anymore

Some older shower valves are no longer manufactured, making replacement more practical than continuing to chase parts for a repair.

Persistent leaking behind the wall

A leak that keeps returning despite repair attempts sometimes means the valve or drain assembly itself needs full replacement, not another patch.

Switching from a tub to a walk-in shower

A tub-to-shower conversion is a common update for households prioritizing accessibility or simply preferring a shower-only bathroom.

Water temperature swings unexpectedly

A shower that suddenly runs hot or cold when another fixture is used elsewhere in the house usually means the valve lacks pressure-balancing, which current code requires for new installations.

Planning an accessibility upgrade

A curbless shower entry or added grab-bar blocking behind the wall is easier and cheaper to plan for during a full installation than to retrofit later.

Local factors

What we check on older Torrance bathrooms

Bathrooms in Old Torrance and Walteria homes built before pressure-balancing valves became standard often still have older single-handle or two-handle valves that don't compensate for pressure changes elsewhere in the house, which current California plumbing code requires for new shower and tub-shower installations.

We also check the condition of supply lines and the drain assembly behind the wall during any shower or tub project, since it's far more practical to address an aging shutoff valve or corroded fitting while the wall is already open than to reopen it again later.

Many Craftsman bungalows in Old Torrance have compact original bathrooms, and remodels there usually mean opening walls that have not been touched in decades. Converting a tub to a walk-in shower changes the drain location, so we check the floor framing before the new drain is set.

How we diagnose it

How a shower or tub installation works

Scope confirmation

We confirm whether the project is a like-for-like fixture swap, a valve replacement, or a full tub-to-shower conversion, since each involves different plumbing work.

Valve and supply line work

Old valves are replaced with current, pressure-balancing or thermostatic-mixing valves as required by code.

Drain reconfiguration if needed

A tub-to-shower conversion or fixture relocation may require moving or reconfiguring the drain assembly.

Pressure test and verification

New connections are pressure tested and checked for leaks before any tile or surround work proceeds.

Working with your contractor

How we coordinate with tile and remodel work

Shower and tub plumbing is usually one part of a larger bathroom remodel involving a tile setter, and we coordinate scheduling so the plumbing rough-in and final connections happen at the right points in that timeline, rather than holding up the rest of the project.

If you already have a general contractor managing the remodel, we're glad to work directly with them on scheduling and specifications rather than requiring you to relay information between us.

We also handle the rough-in inspection step that most jurisdictions, including Torrance, require before the wall is closed up. Scheduling that inspection at the right point in the project, after the valve and drain are set but before tile goes up, avoids the far more disruptive alternative of reopening a finished wall.

Bath projects

New tubs, shower valves, and jetted tubs

A new tub or shower is a plumbing job as much as a finishing one. The drain, the valve, and the supply lines all need to line up. Tell the dispatcher what is being removed, what is going in, and whether walls are open.

Jetted tubs have pumps and jets that can fail. If yours is not working, mention the model so we can plan a repair. If a valve or showerhead is leaking, our fixture repair page covers the quicker fixes.

FAQ

Questions about shower and bathtub installation in Torrance

Do you do the tile and surround work too, or just plumbing?

We handle the plumbing side, valve, supply lines, and drain. We're glad to coordinate with a tile or remodel contractor handling the rest.

Can you convert my tub to a walk-in shower?

Yes, tub-to-shower conversions are a common project, and we handle the plumbing reconfiguration involved.

Do older shower valves need to be replaced, or can they be repaired?

It depends on the valve. Some can be repaired with updated internals. Others, especially very old models, are more practical to replace outright.

Who schedules the rough-in inspection?

We do, coordinating the timing so it happens after the valve and drain work is complete but before the wall is closed up for tile, keeping the project on schedule.

Can you install a tub or shower in a bathroom I am remodeling?

Yes. Share the layout and whether the walls are open. The technician plans the drain, valve, and supply lines around your remodel.

Can you repair a jetted bathtub?

Often, yes. Jetted tubs have pumps and jets that can fail. Tell the dispatcher the brand and what is not working.

Why does my shower valve leak inside the wall?

A failed seal or a cross-threaded fitting can leak behind the wall. Finding the source before opening the wall saves repair costs.
