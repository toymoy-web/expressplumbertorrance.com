Signs

Signs your home needs a full repipe

Repeated pinhole leaks

More than one or two pinhole leaks in copper supply lines within a couple of years usually means the pipe itself is failing, not just an isolated spot.

Low water pressure throughout the house

Corrosion narrowing pipe diameter from the inside is a common cause of pressure loss that affects every fixture, not just one.

Discolored water from multiple taps

Rust-colored or cloudy water from several fixtures, not just the water heater, often points to failing galvanized supply lines.

Original galvanized pipe still in place

Homes built before the 1960s, including many in Old Torrance, may still have original galvanized steel supply lines well past their expected service life.

Local factors

Why repiping comes up more often in Torrance

Homes in Old Torrance and parts of Walteria that date to the city's earlier growth periods were often built with galvanized steel supply lines, a standard material at the time but one that corrodes from the inside over decades, gradually narrowing the pipe and eventually failing outright. Later copper installations hold up better but are still subject to pinhole leaks over a long enough service life, and to faster corrosion where marine-layer humidity reaches exposed sections near the coast.

Because these failures tend to happen gradually and in more than one spot, a homeowner who's called for the same kind of leak repair two or three times in a few years is often better served financially by a full repipe than by continuing to chase individual failures one at a time as they appear.

Gramercy and Andreo avenues in Old Torrance were Torrance's first residential streets in 1912, and Walteria's older bungalows are close behind in age. Repiping a house of that vintage is planned around the walls that must open, and because many Craftsman homes have plaster worth protecting, we map access points before any cutting begins.

The choice

PEX vs. copper for a repipe

PEX

  • Lower material and labor cost
  • Flexible, fewer joints needed
  • Resistant to the corrosion that affects metal pipe
  • Faster installation, less wall opening

Copper

  • Longer track record in older homes
  • Higher heat tolerance
  • Higher upfront material cost
  • More joints, more potential leak points over time
Feeling the cost of one-off repairs

"I keep calling for the same kind of leak"

Many Torrance homeowners reach the repiping conversation after several separate leak repairs, not because anyone told them to consider it upfront. If that sounds familiar, it's worth an honest look at whether the pipe material itself, not any one repair, is the real underlying issue.

I've had three leaks in two years in different spots. Does that mean I need a repipe?

It's a strong signal. Multiple unrelated leaks usually means the pipe material is failing broadly, not that you've had unusually bad luck with individual fittings.

Is repiping only for very old homes?

Not necessarily. While it's most common in homes with original galvanized lines, copper repiping is sometimes recommended in newer homes with unusually aggressive water chemistry or manufacturing defects in the original pipe.

What to expect

How a repipe actually happens

A whole-home repipe doesn't mean opening every wall in the house. Modern repiping techniques route new lines through attics, crawlspaces, and a limited number of access points, minimizing how much drywall needs to be cut and patched. We coordinate the drywall patching or hand it off cleanly to a patch-and-paint contractor, depending on what you prefer.

Most residential repipes are completed in a few days, with water shut off only for short windows during the actual line swaps rather than for the entire project.

Deciding to repipe

Repipe, partial replacement, or a targeted repair

A repipe replaces the supply lines through the whole house. It makes sense when leaks keep returning, water looks rusty, or pressure has dropped because pipes have narrowed from corrosion. A partial replacement covers one run, like the hot water lines or an attic section.

Do not decide before the cause is confirmed. A single leak can be a one-time repair, and our leak detection shows whether the problem is local or widespread. If the leak is under the floor, slab leak repair may be all you need.

Pipe materials

Galvanized, CPVC, and older copper

Galvanized steel rusts from inside. You may notice reduced flow and brown water well before a burst. CPVC can turn brittle with age and crack at fittings. Copper can develop pinholes in some water conditions, which is why a leak at one spot can be a warning about the rest.

Tell the dispatcher what material you think you have and where the pipes run, such as through the attic or under a slab. That helps us plan access. Replacement is quoted after an inspection, not guessed from the phone.

FAQ

Questions about repiping in Torrance

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Most residential repipes are completed within a few days, depending on the home's size and layout.

Will I be without water during the repipe?

Water is only shut off during short windows while specific sections are connected, not for the entire project.

Do you patch the walls afterward?

We coordinate drywall patching as part of the project, or can work alongside a patch-and-paint contractor you prefer.

When should I repipe instead of patching?

Repeated leaks, discolored water, and falling pressure are all signs that the pipes are failing as a system. If you only have one leak and the rest of the pipe is sound, a repair is usually enough.

How can I tell I have galvanized pipes?

Galvanized pipes are gray steel with threaded joints, and they rust from inside. Reduced pressure and brown water are common signs. A technician can confirm the material quickly.

Can you replace only my hot water pipes?

Often, yes. A partial replacement is possible when the cold lines are in better shape. We inspect both and tell you which makes more sense.

Do CPVC pipes need to be replaced?

Not always. CPVC can become brittle with age, so if it is cracking at fittings, replacement may be wise. A technician checks its condition first.
