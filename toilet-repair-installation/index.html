Signs

Common toilet problems we handle

Toilet runs constantly

A toilet that keeps running after flushing usually has a worn flapper or fill valve, and wastes a meaningful amount of water until it's fixed.

Water pooling at the base

Water around the base of the toilet often means the wax ring seal has failed, which can lead to floor damage if left unaddressed.

Weak or incomplete flush

A flush that doesn't fully clear the bowl can be caused by a clogged trap, mineral buildup in the jets, or a failing flush valve.

Toilet rocks or feels loose

A toilet that shifts when you sit down usually means the mounting bolts have loosened or the wax seal beneath it needs replacement.

An overflowing or constantly leaking toilet is an emergency. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water, then call our 24/7 emergency line, and a technician can be dispatched any hour to clear the clog or replace the failed part.

Local factors

What we see most often in Torrance homes

Older toilets in Old Torrance and Walteria homes are frequently pre-1994 models that use significantly more water per flush than current fixtures, and their internal components have often outlived their expected service life. Mineral buildup from the area's hard water also affects fill valves and flush jets over time, sometimes causing weak flushes even in a relatively young toilet.

For homes considering a remodel or fixture upgrade, a newer high-efficiency toilet can cut water use meaningfully compared to an older unit, something worth factoring in alongside simple repair when a toilet is already due for replacement.

From Craftsman bungalows in Old Torrance to mid-century homes in Southwood, Torrance bathrooms were built in different eras, and so were toilet flange heights and drain locations. We measure the rough-in before choosing a replacement so the new toilet sits level and seals properly against the flange.

How we diagnose it

How we handle a toilet repair call

Symptom diagnosis

We identify whether the issue is the fill valve, flapper, flush valve, wax ring, or the toilet itself.

Component repair

Most running or weak-flush issues are resolved by repairing or replacing the specific failed component.

Wax ring and mounting check

For a toilet that rocks or leaks at the base, we reset the wax ring and confirm the mounting bolts are properly secured.

Flange condition check

We inspect the flange itself, since a cracked or improperly set flange is a common reason a toilet keeps leaking even after the wax ring is replaced.

Full replacement when it makes sense

If the toilet itself is old enough or damaged enough that repair isn't cost-effective, we'll say so directly and walk through replacement options.

Installation

New toilet installation done right

A new toilet installation involves more than setting a new fixture on the existing flange. We check the flange itself for damage or improper height, since a bad flange connection is a common cause of leaks and wobbling on a supposedly new installation, not just old fixtures.

We also make sure the shutoff valve and supply line are in good working condition before connecting a new toilet, since replacing a decades-old, corroded shutoff valve at the same time avoids a second service call down the road.

For a bathroom remodel, we can also install a comfort-height or water-efficient model in place of an older standard toilet, since current fixtures use meaningfully less water per flush than units made before the mid-1990s without sacrificing flush performance.

By symptom

Running, leaking, or weak flush: what each one means

Runs on its own every few minutes

A flapper that does not seal lets water trickle into the bowl. It can raise a bill quietly, so it is worth fixing.

Water around the base

A failing wax ring or loose closet bolts let water seep out. Left alone, it can soften the floor beneath.

Handle feels loose or does nothing

A broken lever or chain is a quick repair. Tell us if the tank is a one-piece model.

Gurgles after flushing

Bubbling often means a partial drain blockage or a venting issue rather than a toilet fault.

A toilet that runs randomly in two bathrooms usually means two worn flappers, not a house leak. If the bill has jumped and you cannot find the cause, our leak detection team can check the rest of the plumbing.

New toilets

Replacing a toilet, including the old one

A replacement is more than setting a new bowl. The old toilet has to come out, the flange and floor need a look, and the new bowl must fit the rough-in. Elongated and round bowls are not interchangeable in every space, so measure or tell us what you have.

Repeat clogs can mean a drain problem rather than a toilet problem. If so, our drain cleaning service looks at the line itself. For bidet seats and other add-ons, mention the brand and model when you call so the right parts are on the truck.

FAQ

Questions about toilet repair in Torrance

Can you install a toilet I've already purchased?

Yes, we can install a customer-supplied toilet, and will check the flange, shutoff valve, and supply line as part of the installation.

Is it worth repairing an old toilet or should I replace it?

It depends on the toilet's age and the specific issue. We'll give you an honest assessment rather than defaulting to whichever costs more.

How long does toilet installation take?

Most single toilet installations or replacements are completed within an hour or two.

Will a new toilet actually save water compared to my old one?

Often significantly. Toilets made before the mid-1990s can use more than twice the water per flush of a current standard model, which adds up meaningfully over a year of normal household use.

How can I tell if the wax ring has failed?

Water at the base of the toilet after flushing, a faint sewer smell, or a toilet that rocks slightly are the usual signs. Replacing the ring means lifting the toilet, so the flange gets inspected at the same time.

Why does my toilet run on its own?

A worn flapper or a stuck fill valve lets water slip into the bowl and refill the tank. It can add up on your water bill, so it is a repair worth doing sooner than later.

Do you remove the old toilet when you replace it?

Removal is part of the job to plan for, so ask the dispatcher when you book. They confirm exactly what the visit covers before sending a technician.

Can you work on a toilet with a bidet seat?

Often, yes. Tell the dispatcher the brand and what is not working so the technician arrives with a realistic plan.

Why does my toilet gurgle after I flush?

Gurgling usually comes from a partial blockage somewhere downstream, not from the toilet itself. A camera look tells us whether the cause is the drain or the vent.
