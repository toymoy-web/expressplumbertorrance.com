A planned community

A 1923 plan that still shapes the streets

Palos Verdes Estates began in 1923 as the first subdivision of the Palos Verdes Project, 3,200 acres carved from the old Rancho de los Palos Verdes and laid out under landscape architect Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. and the Olmsted Brothers firm. It became the Peninsula's first city in 1939 and has about 13,300 residents today.

Its Mediterranean Revival town centers, including Malaga Cove Plaza, which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and the Malaga Cove Library, keep the community looking much as it was planned. Like the other Palos Verdes cities, it has strong architecture and development committees that tightly regulate building. Torrance Beach ends at Malaga Cove, so the northern edge of Palos Verdes Estates adjoins the Torrance shoreline.

Why trenchless water line work leads here

Protecting an Olmsted landscape while fixing what is underground

Mature planting and parkways

Trees, parkways, and planted yards are part of what the community was designed around. Trenchless replacement needs only small access pits, which leaves far more of that landscaping in place than an open trench would.

Homes from the first decades

The first subdivision dates to the 1920s, so some homes and their service lines are approaching a century old and are worth inspecting before a remodel.

Ocean-facing exposure

Properties at Malaga Cove and Lunada Bay catch marine humidity and salt air, which speeds corrosion on exposed fittings, hose bibs, and garage connections.

Emergency dispatch around the clock

Hillside streets above the ocean are no place to wait out a failed line, so our emergency line takes Palos Verdes Estates calls at any hour, every day of the year.

What a Palos Verdes Estates visit looks like

Palos Verdes Estates is a few miles southwest of our Torrance base, reached by Palos Verdes Boulevard and Palos Verdes Drive West. Malaga Cove Plaza and the Malaga Cove Library are easy reference points when a customer is describing where a property sits on the hill.

Coverage here includes the surrounding ZIP codes, including Torrance (90505, 90503), Redondo Beach (90277), and Rancho Palos Verdes (90275), for water line repair, leak detection, and repiping.

Water comes from California Water Service, which has served the Palos Verdes area since 1970 and buys imported supply for the Peninsula, so it is a different utility from Torrance Municipal Water even though regional hardness is similar.

Most plumbing inside a home does not touch the community's design review, but exterior equipment, visible vents, and changes to hardscape can. We flag it at the estimate stage when a permit or review step is likely, so it does not hold up the job.

Local FAQ

Questions from Palos Verdes Estates residents

Will repairing a water line damage my landscaping?

Not necessarily. Trenchless replacement uses small access pits at each end instead of a long open trench, so most mature planting, walkways, and parkway trees stay where they are.

Do design review rules affect plumbing work in Palos Verdes Estates?

Interior plumbing generally does not, but changes to exterior equipment, visible vents, or hardscape can. We tell you at the estimate stage when a review or permit step is likely.

Who supplies water in Palos Verdes Estates?

California Water Service serves the city through its Palos Verdes district. Torrance, just to the north, is mostly on its own municipal system, though regional hardness is similar.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Palos Verdes Estates?

Yes. The city uses a single ZIP code, 90274, which it shares with Rolling Hills, and we also cover the neighboring Torrance, Redondo Beach, and Rancho Palos Verdes ZIP codes.

Is emergency service available in Palos Verdes Estates?

Yes, our 24/7 emergency line covers Palos Verdes Estates the same as the rest of our service area, including nights, weekends, and holidays.
