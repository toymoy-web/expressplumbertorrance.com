The call we get most from Lomita

Clogged drains and aging sewer lines

More than anything else, Lomita calls us about drains and sewer lines that clog and keep clogging. It's a small city, just over a square mile, built out mostly in the 1940s and 50s with closely spaced single-family lots, and a lot of the original clay and cast-iron sewer laterals from that era are still in the ground, now old enough that root intrusion and gradual cracking are common.

Recurring clogs

A drain that clears with a snake and clogs again within weeks usually means root intrusion in an old lateral, not a one-off blockage.

Small lots, close plumbing runs

Lomita's tightly spaced lots mean sewer laterals often run close to mature trees planted decades ago, a direct contributor to root problems.

Sewage odor near the yard cleanout

A persistent smell near a cleanout is a common early sign of a cracked or partially blocked lateral in this city's older housing stock.

Emergency dispatch around the clock

A sewer backup does not wait for morning, and our emergency line covers Lomita any hour of the day or night.

Hydro jetting as the actual fix

Because snaking alone tends not to hold in Lomita's older lines, hydro jetting is a more common long-term answer here than in newer construction.

Lomita's housing and layout

One of the smallest cities in the South Bay

Lomita sits directly between Torrance and Harbor City, and is home to the Lomita Railroad Museum on 250th Street, a real landmark most residents pass regularly. The city's housing is smaller and older on average than much of surrounding Torrance, which is part of why drain and sewer issues show up here at a higher rate relative to the city's size.

Our Lomita coverage extends to the surrounding ZIP codes as well, including Harbor City (90710), the neighboring Torrance ZIPs (90501, 90502), and Rolling Hills Estates (90274), for drain cleaning, sewer line repair, and hydro jetting service.

What a service call actually looks like

How we approach an old Lomita sewer line

On a first call for a recurring clog in Lomita, we lean toward a camera inspection before recommending another round of snaking, precisely because the pattern is so familiar in this city: a line clears, then backs up again within a few weeks. Confirming root intrusion on camera means we can recommend hydro jetting or a targeted spot repair with actual evidence, rather than guessing based on the neighborhood's age alone.

Given how small Lomita is geographically, response times here run the same as our core Torrance coverage, and a technician can typically bring jetting equipment on the initial visit rather than needing a separate scheduled trip once a camera confirms the cause.

The Lomita Railroad Museum itself sits in a residential pocket of the city rather than a commercial strip, which is a useful reminder that most of Lomita's plumbing calls come from single-family homes on quiet streets, not the handful of businesses along its main commercial corridors. That residential concentration is part of why we staff Lomita calls the same way we staff our core Torrance routes rather than treating it as an outlying stop.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Lomita

Lomita's older sewer laterals mean a backup can turn into standing water fast. Here is what to do first if you have an active backup or burst pipe.

Shut off the main valve

Stop the water at the source, if you can safely reach the main shutoff.

Kill power to wet areas

If water has reached an outlet or panel, cut power to that area.

Clear the immediate area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the water.

Local FAQ

Questions from Lomita residents

Why does my drain keep clogging even after it's been snaked?

In Lomita's older neighborhoods, this is frequently root intrusion in a clay or cast-iron lateral. Snaking clears the immediate blockage but leaves the roots and the entry point, so it recurs.

Is hydro jetting overkill for a small city like Lomita?

Not usually. Given how common root intrusion is in Lomita's older lateral lines, jetting is often the more cost-effective long-term fix compared to repeated snaking visits.

Is Lomita's water utility the same as Torrance's?

Not exactly. Most of Lomita is served by the City of Lomita's own water system, with a portion served by Cal Water's Palos Verdes district, while most of Torrance is on Torrance Municipal Water. All of them draw on the same regional supply network, so hardness and scale buildup are similar.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Lomita?

Yes, we cover all of Lomita, ZIP code 90717, from one end of the city to the other, plus the immediately surrounding Harbor City and Torrance ZIPs.

Is emergency service available in Lomita?

Yes, our 24/7 emergency line covers Lomita the same as every other city in our service area, including sewer backups and burst pipes after hours.
